ABOUT US

Alise Sepius, Head Executive of IAPETUS
Marq Pellock, Former Founder
Tye Hilfiger, Former Founder

The story of the IAPETUS corporation is as long as it is tragic. Tye and Marq, honourable soldiers who were unrecognized by their government, and hated by civilians for the anti war sentiment brought them to a life of crime. Night Market Jobs that didn't pay them much but was enough to get them coming back, Marq had enough. He figured if they already went this far, why not just get all the money themselves?

When they tried to steal the money from the bank their contacts had stored their money in, catastrophe struck which resulted in the death of five civilians and three police casualities. Though the documents say there was only one documented fatality, all were directly tied to police incompetence, being untrained they dispersed their firearm without regard if they suspected them as 'undercover assets'. The case was rigged against them and they suffered years in prison. Illness spread like miasma through the vents, the treatment was poor, and the abuse had deeply affected Tye, though they were convicts, they were still the people he fought for, and people who were promised rehabilitation. The two watched as inmates died due to negligence, from injury or illness, they begged and plead, but the only response they got was they 'didn't get enough funding'. Marq who was familiar with how Equity Firms held a firm grip of the pharmaceutical market knew there was a seed of truth in it. So they had sought to establish their own firm which could reliably and affordably give out drugs to those in need. It all started with an old house, sold less than the dentures on the indentured man.

Many firms tried to buy them, but they always refused. The people mattered to the two most. As their business steadily went by, Marq, without consulting Tye had invested in research that could cure arthritis without needing long term perscriptions. Tye was deeply upset by what he perceived as a betrayal, but Marq ignored it continued, when they had finally done something actionable for the private equity firms to use them against.

Several cures and antidotes have already been made, but the patents of which have been bought and gatekept by Equity firms to ensure that people would have to pay for sustained survival. The current Head Executive of IAPETUS (Alise Sepius) has gone on record stating that she fully believed in the goals IAPETUS had, and wished to preserve their company, unlike many she had seen that were consumed or dissolved by larger companies. Alise and the two owners got along, having all been in service, just different places different times. She decided to take the legal back home, Tye and Marq bringing their old tactics to new suburban fiends

The Alise and Margrove case was one of the most public rebellions against the pharmaceutical monopolies, which in the small skeptic circles that kept a close eye on supersticion, felt heavily validated and empowered by their willingness to fight. In spite of the fighting, Marq had become so overwhelmed by the paperwork and realizing that he and Tye could not do this, trying to warn Tye that he should quit while he's ahead and give the company to someone he trusted. Tye had reprimanded his brother in arms for being a coward, saying 'The next time I see you, you better be dead!'. Tye was left as the sole proprietor to their growing company.

Tye began to expand rapidly, expending company resources on making more locations, though the store was only a rural success. The company almost faced bankruptcy, and should have- if not for the intervention of Alise Sepius. She had invested some of the firms funds towards IAPETUS. Margrove was upset, in a very well documented incident in a business meeting Margrove said that he did not know Alise, one of the finest lawyers wasting their money on a company that is bound to sink.
